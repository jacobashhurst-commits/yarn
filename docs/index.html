Apple Silicon Mac. No account. Everything stays on this Mac.

Yarn records your meetings, figures out who said what, and turns conversations into searchable memories, actionable notes and a daily brief.

Everything is processed and stored on your Mac. No cloud required and no subscription.

How does it work?

Small brain. Surprisingly useful. (Like its creator.)

Yarn uses free, local AI. It doesn't have the horsepower of ChatGPT or Claude, but you don't need a Ferrari to drive to the shops.

It's a little slower. Who cares?

Yarn works in the background. Record your next meeting while it's processing the last one.

Keep transcripts, not terabytes.

Text takes almost no space. Yarn can delete the audio after a week, after a month, or keep it. The transcript stays.

Getting started

  1. 1

    Record

    Open Yarn from the Dock, or click the Yarn icon in the menu bar.

  2. 2

    Get on with it

    Yarn transcribes and summarises in the background.

  3. 3

    Remember

    Search your meetings or open your daily brief. All locally.

Yarn isn't trying to be ChatGPT or Claude. It doesn't need to be. Ask Yarn stays on this Mac. If you connect Claude, ChatGPT, or Cursor, a search sends that app the passages it asked for.

Not everything needs to be a SaaS. Your conversations are yours. It's nice to keep them that way.

Download, open the zip, and double-click Yarn Setup. It brings its own Python and the rest. You do not need anything installed already.

If the speech-model download stops before it finishes, use the Japan installer. It is about 1.6 GB and the model is already inside.

Click Install Yarn and leave the window open. The first recording asks for the microphone. For a headphone call, click + under Privacy & Security, Screen & System Audio Recording, and choose Yarn from the Applications folder in your home folder.
